Why AI assurance matters

AI creates new opportunities and new security questions

AI applications can process sensitive information, connect to business systems and influence decisions. Organisations need to understand the system's purpose, data, permissions, limitations and failure modes before and after deployment.

  • Understand what information an AI system can access or reveal.
  • Test how it responds to prompt injection and malicious inputs.
  • Review identity, access controls and connected tools.
  • Consider privacy, human oversight and escalation processes.
  • Document findings, residual risks and recommended improvements.

Australian policy context

Understand the standards landscape

We distinguish current voluntary guidance from announced policy intentions and any future legal obligations. The applicability of requirements depends on the organisation, system and final legislation.

Proposed frontier AI protections announced 10 November 2026

The Australian Government announced its intention to legislate six safety and security obligations for frontier AI companies that wish to operate in Australia. Further details are to be developed. This announcement is not itself enacted legislation.

Guidance for AI Adoption

The Department of Industry, Science and Resources publishes guidance describing six essential practices for safe and responsible AI governance and adoption.

Voluntary AI Safety Standard

The Australian Government's voluntary standard provides guardrails for organisations across the AI supply chain. It is guidance and does not, by itself, create new legal duties.

Cyber security and privacy obligations

Existing privacy, consumer, sector-specific and cyber security requirements may apply depending on the organisation and AI use case. Obtain legal advice for interpretation.
